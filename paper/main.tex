\begin{abstract}
This paper evaluates an external deployment layer for adapting a frozen Anticipatory Music Transformer (AMT) checkpoint to turn-based MIDI call-and-response without retraining. The layer combines an onset-density endpoint heuristic, phrase controls, asynchronous playback, and speculative scheduling. Evidence comprises a first-commit replay on 100 Calls, repeated candidate and module runs, a matched-versus-mismatched symbolic feature audit, and a deterministic scheduler replay. The 27,000 and 63,000 trial rows are descriptive; candidate, module, association, and scheduler uncertainty is computed at 100 Call units with an 87-source-cluster sensitivity analysis, whereas endpoint intervals use the 100 Calls only. Controlled AMT raises the structural-compliance composite fixed for the final analysis from 0.560968 to 0.732610 relative to raw AMT (Call-cluster 95\% CI for the difference [0.157901, 0.185151]), while the motif baseline remains higher at 0.752682. Feature association is not uniformly improved: relative to raw AMT, the controlled-minus-raw onset-profile association contrast is +0.020016 (Call-jackknife 95\% CI [0.006927, 0.033105]), whereas the interval-2gram contrast is -0.096715 [-0.113257, -0.080172]; equal-source analyses preserve both directions. Using final Note-Off as a reproducible but non-human boundary proxy, the primary adaptive detector attains a custom premature-sensitive first-commit score (PS-F1) of 0.619 [0.523, 0.711] at a two-second deadline, compared with 0.500 [0.400, 0.590] for a fixed 800 ms threshold; overlapping uncertainty and sensitivity to the heuristic survival-timer threshold preclude a universal-optimum claim. With an 80 ms commit-relative target, a 150 ms pre-commit overlap reduces the modeled first-generated-event readiness lower bound from 101.543 to 86.545 ms (Call-cluster reduction 14.999 ms [10.152, 20.494]) and readiness-target misses from 20.744\% to 5.244\%. The replay does not observe MIDI transmission or audio onset. No human-participant data are analyzed or reported, and no live co-performance study or human phrase-boundary annotation is included. The evidence supports an inspectable engineering adaptation and narrow structural manipulation/association checks, not musical quality, human-responsive turn taking, or perceptual superiority.
\end{abstract}


\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} symbolic music generation; MIDI; call-and-response; Transformer; endpoint detection; turn-based interaction; objective evaluation; clustered inference; speculative preload

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Call-and-response is a foundational pattern in live improvisation. A computational system must not only generate a plausible continuation; it must decide when the Call has ended, return events on a performance clock, and recover safely from unusable output.

AMT already supports controlled anticipation and infilling \citep{thickstun2023amt}; the deployment question examined here is narrower. A frozen checkpoint does not by itself define turn boundaries, cancellation semantics, MIDI scheduling, or application-specific phrase constraints. The implemented wrapper addresses these engineering requirements, but the present experiments replay files rather than observe musicians interacting with the system.

Three research questions organize the evidence: \textbf{RQ1}, how does the endpoint heuristic trade premature commits against delay under a reproducible file-end proxy? \textbf{RQ2}, how do cumulative external controls change a declared output-compliance score and narrow Call--response feature associations relative to raw AMT and a motif baseline? \textbf{RQ3}, how much can speculative preload reduce a modeled first-generated-event readiness lower bound in deterministic replay? The contribution is the inspectable adaptation architecture and its claim-separated evaluation. Human boundary validity, live co-performance usefulness, and perceptual response quality remain future tests.


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

This section reviews the four areas that shape the proposed system: symbolic music representations, generative sequence models, real-time co-performance systems, and evaluation of generative music.

\subsection{Symbolic Music Representation}


Early symbolic music generation often used piano-rolls or fixed time-step grids, but rigid quantization can create sparse matrices and discard micro-timing such as rubato and swing \citep{boulanger2012modeling}. Event streams offer an alternative \citep{oore2020feeling,huang2018musictransformer}. AMT represents notes with quantized arrival time, duration, and instrument--pitch tokens and supports asynchronous control events \citep{thickstun2023amt}. Section~\ref{sec:relative-interval-nucleus-sampling} reports the exact checkpoint and adapter mapping used here; it is not a relative pitch-interval representation.

\subsection{Sequence Models for Music Generation}

Symbolic music generation has moved from memoryless or recurrent models such as LSTMs \citep{eck2002finding} to self-attention-based Transformer architectures \citep{vaswani2017attention,huang2018musictransformer}. AMT extends this line toward controllable temporal-event generation and anticipation \citep{thickstun2023amt}. Accordingly, the novelty claimed here is not that AMT is converted from a purely offline model into an interactive one. It is the turn-taking, cancellation, phrase-control, and scheduling layer around one pretrained AMT checkpoint.

\subsection{Real-Time Human-AI Co-Performance}

Real-time human--computer improvisation has a substantial pre-Transformer history. The Continuator learns style during interaction and produces stylistically related continuations \citep{pachet2003continuator}; OMax uses learned sequence structure for improvisation \citep{assayag2006omax}; ImproteK introduces scenario-guided improvisation \citep{nika2016improtek}; and Somax2 frames machine listening, memory, and generation as cocreative interaction \citep{assayag2022somax2}. MIDI-performance research emphasizes timing stability \citep{nelson2004midi}; Scramble Live combines learned models and live control \citep{privato2022scramble}; and recent work couples Max/MSP front ends with Python generative back ends \citep{karchkhadze2026towards}. Relative to these systems, the present work focuses on a reproducible wrapper for a frozen arrival-time Transformer: revocable endpoint candidates, explicit control modules, a queue-based scheduler, and a first-event readiness lower-bound replay. It does not establish superior musical interaction.

\subsection{Evaluation of Generative Music}

While human listening tests remain vital for evaluating musical quality, objective metrics are useful for reproducible structural analysis but cannot replace perceptual or human-centered evaluation \citep{ji2023survey,lerch2025evaluation}. For call-and-response, output-only rule compliance, conditional correspondence to the Call, phrase-boundary validity, scheduler timing, and live performer experience are different constructs. The present evaluation measures the first, three narrow symbolic feature associations for the second, and engineering proxies for the third and fourth. It contains no harmonic-fit, human-perceived correspondence, governed listening, or live-user evaluation.

\section{Point-Process-Inspired Timing Heuristic and Autoregressive Generation}
\label{sec:temporal-modeling}

\subsection{Continuous-Time MIDI Event Representation}

The performance is represented as a continuous-time event sequence
\begin{equation}
E=\{e_i=(t_i,d_i,p_i)\}_{i=1}^{N},\qquad
0\leq t_1\leq\cdots\leq t_N,
\end{equation}
where $t_i$ is onset time, $d_i>0$ is duration, and $p_i\in\{0,\ldots,127\}$ is MIDI pitch. Non-strict onset ordering permits chords; Section~\ref{sec:midi-vad-engineering} clusters near-simultaneous Note-On events before estimating onset density.

\subsection{Conditional Autoregressive Factorization}

For a human Call $C$ and response $R=\{e_j^r\}_{j=1}^{n}$, the frozen AMT decoder uses
\begin{equation}
P(R\mid C)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}P(e_j^r\mid C,e_{<j}^r),
\end{equation}
with event-level factorization
\begin{equation}
P(e_j^r\mid\cdot)=P(t_j^r\mid\cdot)
P(d_j^r\mid t_j^r,\cdot)
P(p_j^r\mid t_j^r,d_j^r,\cdot).
\end{equation}
This event-level factorization is conceptual: the implementation decodes the checkpoint's interleaved token triplets rather than fitting three separate probability models. Endpoint detection, scheduling, and phrase controls remain external and do not retrain its parameters.

\subsection{Dynamic Turn Segmentation Based on Continuous-Time Stopping Time}

Let $T_{\mathrm{last}}$ be the most recent Note-On and $\tau=t-T_{\mathrm{last}}$ the current silence. A point-process survival expression motivates the monotone mapping from recent onset density to a waiting time \citep{daley2003pointprocesses}:
\begin{equation}
S(\tau)=\exp\left[-\int_0^{\tau}\lambda(T_{\mathrm{last}}+u)\,du\right].
\end{equation}
For the short online window, the implementation uses a local constant-rate approximation. If $u_1,\ldots,u_{K_{\mathrm{on}}}$ are up to the eight most recent clustered onset times and $\mu_{\min}=0.25\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\mathrm{tempo}}=
\begin{cases}
\mu_{\min}, & K_{\mathrm{on}}<2\ \text{or}\ u_{K_{\mathrm{on}}}\leq u_1,\\
\max\left(\mu_{\min},\dfrac{K_{\mathrm{on}}-1}{u_{K_{\mathrm{on}}}-u_1}\right), & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
S(\tau)=\exp(-\mu_{\mathrm{tempo}}\tau).
\end{equation}
With heuristic survival-timer threshold $\theta=0.05$, the candidate cutoff and stopping condition are
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{cutoff}}=\frac{\ln(1/\theta)}{\mu_{\mathrm{tempo}}},
\label{eq:dynamic-cutoff}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
t-T_{\mathrm{last}}>\tau_{\mathrm{cutoff}}.
\label{eq:endpoint-condition}
\end{equation}
Thus the silence allowance shortens as recent onset density rises. The primary setting uses no lower or upper cutoff clamp; at the floor intensity, $\tau_{\mathrm{cutoff}}=\ln(20)/0.25\approx11.98$ s. Section~\ref{subsec:endpoint-benchmark} therefore includes 2 s and 4 s upper-clamp sensitivities. The implementation does not fit a point-process likelihood, estimate a calibrated hazard, or infer musical syntax. The equations define a point-process-inspired engineering timer, not a stochastic generative model or proof of phrase completion. Chord correction, revocable confirmation, and the proxy benchmark define its operational behavior.


\section{Real-Time MIDI Call-and-Response System Design}
\label{sec:system-design}

\subsection{Cross-Environment Architecture Based on Virtual MIDI }

The system uses a modular Virtual MIDI Bus architecture rather than a monolithic offline-generation pipeline. Physical MIDI input, host audio rendering, neural inference, and MIDI output scheduling are decoupled so that equivalent routing chains can be used across operating systems and digital audio workstations.

In Windows, the routing chain can use loopMIDI with REAPER, Decent Sampler, or another host; in macOS, it can use the IAC Bus with Logic Pro or an equivalent DAW. The system is therefore defined by the interaction chain itself: physical MIDI input, virtual MIDI routing, a Python inference engine, and host-based rendering.

The online path is therefore capture $\rightarrow$ candidate/commit $\rightarrow$ AMT decoding $\rightarrow$ MIDI playback. Speculative work may begin at a candidate, but only committed Calls enter the audible path; canceled work is discarded.

\subsection{Engineering Implementation of the Intelligent MIDI Endpoint Detection Module}
\label{sec:midi-vad-engineering}

The MIDI-VAD daemon polls Note-On events, updates the clustered onset window, and evaluates Equations~\eqref{eq:dynamic-cutoff}--\eqref{eq:endpoint-condition}. Its state machine is
\begin{equation}
\text{Listening}
\rightarrow
\text{Candidate}
\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{Cancel} \rightarrow \text{Listening},\\
\text{Commit} \rightarrow \text{Inference}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:midi-vad-state-machine}
\end{equation}
where a new Note-On cancels an unconfirmed candidate; commit freezes the Call for inference.

\subsubsection{Chord-Clustering Correction for Onset-Density Estimation}

Counting every note in a chord would inflate $\mu_{\mathrm{tempo}}$. Note-On events within
\begin{equation}
\Delta t_{\mathrm{cluster}} \approx 0.08\,\mathrm{s}.
\label{eq:cluster-window}
\end{equation}
are merged into one onset cluster. The value is an engineering setting, not a learned constant. Online accompaniment work explicitly allows imperfect performance detection \citep{dannenberg1984online}; related MIDI voice separation groups onsets within 35 ms and separately uses an 80 ms busy-note allowance after onset-group time \citep{madsen2006separating}. Neither value transfers directly to this detector. Here 80 ms corrects density only, and Section~\ref{subsec:endpoint-benchmark} tests 0, 40, 80, and 120 ms sensitivity rather than treating it as universal.

\subsubsection{Two-Stage Revocable Candidate Endpoint and Confirmation Window}
\label{sec:revocable-endpoint}

When Equation~\eqref{eq:endpoint-condition} is first satisfied, the system records $T_{\mathrm{candidate}}$ and waits
\begin{equation}
\Delta T_{\mathrm{confirm}} \approx 0.15\,\mathrm{s}.
\label{eq:confirm-window}
\end{equation}
A Note-On within this interval cancels the candidate; otherwise it commits. The 150 ms value is not a perceptual threshold. Synchronous music can demand roughly 20--30 ms latency \citep{lago2004quest}, while tolerance varies strongly with task and feedback topology \citep{rottondi2016overview}. For this turn-taking system, 150 ms is a debounce/latency compromise tested against 0, 75, and 250 ms in Section~\ref{subsec:endpoint-benchmark}; preload can overlap, but not extend beyond, this same interval.
\subsection{Asynchronous Streaming Decoding and Micro-Buffered Scheduling}
\label{sec:streaming-decoding}

An inference thread places playable events in a thread-safe queue while a playback thread converts them to MIDI. The scheduler uses an 80 ms startup target anchored to endpoint commit. Let $a_j$ be the scheduled onset of generated event $j$, let $C_j=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\tau_{\mathrm{infer}}(k)$ be the time at which that event becomes ready relative to inference start, let $H$ be the elapsed preload horizon before endpoint commit, and let $B=80$ ms be the commit-relative target. Playback starts, relative to commit, at
\begin{equation}
s=\max(B,C_1-H).
\label{eq:commit-anchored-start}
\end{equation}
The event-readiness condition is
\begin{equation}
C_j-H \leq s+(a_j-a_1),
\qquad \forall j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.
\label{eq:streaming-buffer-condition}
\end{equation}
Unlike the previous duration-sum expression, Equation~\eqref{eq:streaming-buffer-condition} compares decoder readiness with scheduled onset deadlines. Note durations do not accumulate playback availability; Note-Off messages are scheduled independently. The released replay stores the elapsed time until the generator first yields a \texttt{GeneratedEvent} (under the legacy field name \path{first_token_latency_sec}) and total-generation time, but not every $C_j$. It therefore estimates only a first-event readiness lower bound and cannot estimate MIDI-send time, audio onset, or full-stream underrun.

\subsubsection{Speculative Preload for Candidate Endpoints}
\label{sec:speculative-preload}

At candidate time, the engine snapshots the Call and may start decoding while MIDI monitoring continues. A commit reuses available tokens; a cancel discards them. The scheduler model used in Section~\ref{subsec:latency-logging} is
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{first}}(H)
=
\max\left(
B,\,
\tau_{\mathrm{first}}-H
\right).
\label{eq:perceived-latency-preload}
\end{equation}
Here $\tau_{\mathrm{first}}$ is elapsed inference time until the first generated event object is yielded, $B=80$ ms, and $H\in\{0,150\}$ ms is pre-commit overlap. Without preload, $R_{\mathrm{first}}(0)=\max(B,\tau_{\mathrm{first}})$. Because $\Delta T_{\mathrm{preload}}=\Delta T_{\mathrm{confirm}}=150$ ms, no 250 ms post-confirmation horizon exists: preload occupies only the revocable candidate interval. Canceled candidates trade temporary computation for a lower modeled readiness bound. Phrase-level duration/style processing, queueing, operating-system MIDI transmission, host rendering, and audio onset occur later and are not timed by this replay.

\subsection{Frozen AMT Checkpoint, Arrival-Time Encoding, and Sampling}
\label{sec:relative-interval-nucleus-sampling}

The evaluated model is the frozen \texttt{stanford-crfm/music-small-800k} checkpoint: the 128M-parameter small AMT trained for 800,000 steps on arrival-time-encoded Lakh MIDI \citep{stanfordcrfm2023musicsmall,thickstun2023amt}. The resolved local revision is \path{fa800530aa1126dd6b58b38f3bdb8fcec9c9d4f5}; its configuration has 12 layers, 12 attention heads, hidden size 768, a 1,024-token context, and vocabulary size 55,028.

Each captured note is converted to three integer-valued fields,
\begin{equation}
\left(
q_i^{t},q_i^{d},q_i^{n}
\right)
=
\left(
\operatorname{round}(100t_i),
\max[1,\operatorname{round}(100d_i)],
128\,\iota_i+p_i
\right),
\label{eq:relative-arrival-time}
\end{equation}
where $\iota_i$ is the instrument index (fixed to zero for captured Calls). Vocabulary offsets convert the fields into arrival-time, duration, and instrument--pitch tokens, after which AMT's event sorter orders the stream. Velocity is not encoded by this adapter; generated events use the playback default 92. Generation prepends the \texttt{AUTOREGRESS} control, retains at most 339 events (1,017 event tokens), and re-bases arrival times only when that history window is truncated. Pitch remains absolute, so the earlier description in terms of $\Delta p$ was incorrect.

The decoder applies temperature-scaled nucleus sampling \citep{holtzman2020curious}. For sorted token probabilities $P_T(x_{(i)})$, it retains the smallest prefix satisfying
\begin{equation}
K_{\mathrm{nuc}} =
\min \left\{
k
\ \middle|\
\sum_{i=1}^{k} P_T(x_{(i)}) \geq p
\right\}.
\label{eq:nucleus-k}
\end{equation}
where $p$ is preset-dependent (typically 0.95) and
\begin{equation}
P_T(x_i)
=
\frac{\exp(z_i/T)}
{\sum_j \exp(z_j/T)}.
\label{eq:temperature-scaling}
\end{equation}
These controls reshape sampling but impose no tonal rule; tonal and metrical projection, when enabled, occurs later in the explicit style layer.
\subsection{Phrase-level Music Control Layer}
\label{sec:phrase-level-control}

The phrase controller derives global limits from the Call, rejects repetition failures during sampling, matches response duration, and supplies a deterministic fallback only when no playable neural output exists. It remains external to AMT parameters.

\subsubsection{CallProfile-based Phrase Intention Analysis and ResponsePlan Construction}
\label{sec:callprofile-responseplan}

After commit, the CallProfile is
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{call}}
=
\left[
T_{\mathrm{call}},
N_{\mathrm{call}},
\Omega_{\mathrm{pitch}},
\bar{p}_{\mathrm{call}},
H_{\mathrm{pitch}},
\nabla_{\mathrm{melody}},
\rho_{\mathrm{rhythm}},
T_{\mathrm{repeat}}
\right]^T.
\label{eq:callprofile-vector}
\end{equation}
The terms encode duration, note count, pitch range and mean, pitch-class distribution, first-order contour, onset density, and repeated-tail status. They produce
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Plan}_{\mathrm{resp}}
=
\left\{
T_{\mathrm{target}},
N_{\mathrm{target}},
\Omega_{\mathrm{allow}},
CPR_{\mathrm{limit}},
Share_{\max},
K_{\mathrm{resample}}
\right\}.
\label{eq:responseplan}
\end{equation}
which fixes target duration/note count, allowed register, continuous-repetition and dominant-pitch limits, and the resampling budget.

\subsubsection{Repetition-tail Compression and Repetition Suppression}
\label{sec:repetition-tail-suppression}

A repeated Call tail is flagged either by a final run of at least four equal pitches or by one pitch occurring at least four times and occupying at least 70\% of the notes whose onsets fall in the final 1.5 s. From the first flagged occurrence onward, prompt cleaning retains at most two occurrences of the flagged pitch while preserving every other note and the original order. During decoding, a token is rejected if it would exceed either $CPR_{\mathrm{limit}}$ consecutive equal pitches or dominant-pitch share $Share_{\max}$. Rejection is retried up to $K_{\mathrm{resample}}$ times; exhaustion invokes a nearby consonant event-level repair around $\bar p_{\mathrm{call}}$. This repair is distinct from the whole-phrase motif fallback below.

\subsubsection{Motif-transformation Fallback and Duration Matching}
\label{sec:fallback-duration-matching}

\noindent\textbf{(1) Whole-phrase fallback trigger and mapping.}
The motif generator is called only when decoding returns zero playable events and fallback is enabled. Rejected individual events instead use the event-level repair described above; they do not replace the phrase. For an empty response, the live system transforms the cleaned Call tail:
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{fallback}}
=
T_{\mathrm{scale}}
\circ T_{\mathrm{clip}}
\circ T_{\mathrm{shift}}
\circ T_{\mathrm{inverse}}
(C_{\mathrm{tail}}).
\label{eq:motif-fallback}
\end{equation}

Here $C_{\mathrm{tail}}$ is the last $\min(N_{\mathrm{call}},N_{\mathrm{target}})$ notes after prompt cleaning. The live fallback preserves their chronological order and maps each $e_i=(t_i,d_i,p_i)$ to
\begin{equation}
\hat{t}_i
=
T_{\mathrm{end}}(C)
+
\lambda
\left(
t_i - t_{\mathrm{tail},0}
\right),
\qquad
\hat{d}_i
=
\mathrm{clip}
\left(
\lambda d_i,\,
d_{\min},\,
d_{\max}
\right),
\label{eq:fallback-time-rule}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm{end}}(C)$ is the end time of the Call, $t_{\mathrm{tail},0}$ is the first onset in the selected tail, and $\epsilon=0.001$ s,
\begin{equation}
\lambda =
\max\left(0.2,\frac{T_{\mathrm{target}}}
{\max(T_{\mathrm{tail}}, \epsilon)}\right).
\label{eq:fallback-scale}
\end{equation}
Pitch is generated by inversion around the Call mean pitch and a small contour shift:
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_i
=
\mathrm{clip}
\left(
2\bar{p}_{\mathrm{call}} - p_i + \delta,\,
p_{\min}^{\mathrm{allow}},\,
p_{\max}^{\mathrm{allow}}
\right).
\label{eq:fallback-pitch-rule}
\end{equation}
For live fallback, $\delta=-5$ semitones for descending Calls and $+5$ otherwise. Durations are clipped to configured minima/maxima; rejected fallback events pass through the same repetition repair before acceptance. The standalone offline motif baseline selects the tail under the fixed experiment seed, uses $\delta\in\{-5,-3,2,3,5,7\}$, applies the same pitch-share safeguards, re-sorts by onset, and then applies the experiment's selected style projection. In the A0--A6 ablation, a fallback inherits only the modules enabled by its variant: A4 remains chromatic/free, A5 applies the base style projection, and A6 additionally applies theory refinements. Section~\ref{subsec:controlled-amt-ablation} reports activation separately from event repairs.

\noindent\textbf{(2) Response duration matching.}
The reported AMT adapter uses $R=100$ ticks/s. It first sorts generated events lexicographically by onset tick, pitch, and duration. With duration ticks clipped to $[4,250]$ (0.04--2.5 s), it computes
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{raw}} = \frac{\max_i\{t_i^r+\operatorname{clip}(d_i^r,4,250)\}-\min_i t_i^r}{R}.
\label{eq:raw-response-duration}
\end{equation}
The plan sets $T_{\mathrm{target}}=\operatorname{clip}(T_{\mathrm{call}},2.0,4.8)$ s and $N_{\mathrm{target}}=\operatorname{clip}(\operatorname{round}N_{\mathrm{call}},4,64)$. During decoding, $T_{\mathrm{target}}$ is the generation horizon and $N_{\mathrm{target}}$ is an accepted-event cap; whichever condition terminates generation first determines the retained count. No post-hoc note insertion or deletion is performed. Thus ``note-count matching'' means Call-derived stopping/capping, not forcing exactly $N_{\mathrm{target}}$ notes.

If $T_{\mathrm{raw}}\in[0.80T_{\mathrm{target}},1.25T_{\mathrm{target}}]$, onsets are unchanged and only duration clipping is applied. For a shorter response, define the first onset $t_0=\min_i t_i^r$, raw onset span $S_{\mathrm{raw}}=\max_i t_i^r-t_0$, integer target span
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{target}}=\max\left\{1,\operatorname{round}\left[R\max(T_{\mathrm{target}}-d_{\min},d_{\min})\right]\right\},
\end{equation}
and normalized onset position
\begin{equation}
q_i=\frac{t_i^r-t_0}{\max(1,S_{\mathrm{raw}})}.
\end{equation}
The short-response branch defines
\begin{align}
\eta &= 1-\min\left(1,\frac{S_{\mathrm{raw}}}{\max(1,S_{\mathrm{target}})}\right),\\
\beta &= \operatorname{clip}(0.35+0.45\eta,0.35,0.80),\\
q_i' &= (1-\beta)q_i+\beta\frac{i}{\max(1,n-1)},
\label{eq:actual-onset-time}
\end{align}
With $\alpha_s=\min\{2,T_{\mathrm{target}}/\max(T_{\mathrm{raw}},0.001)\}$, it then sets
\begin{align}
t_i' &= t_0+\operatorname{round}(q_i'S_{\mathrm{target}}),\\
d_i' &= \operatorname{clip}\left(\operatorname{round}(\alpha_s d_i^r),4,250\right).
\label{eq:actual-duration}
\end{align}
For $T_{\mathrm{raw}}>1.25T_{\mathrm{target}}$, both $(t_i^r-t_0)$ and $d_i^r$ are instead multiplied by $T_{\mathrm{target}}/\max(T_{\mathrm{raw}},0.001)$, rounded, and duration-clipped to the same bounds. All integer conversions use Python's nearest-integer, ties-to-even \texttt{round}; events are re-sorted by onset, pitch, and duration, so onset ties are deterministic. For $n=1$, $i/\max(1,n-1)=0$ and the note keeps the first-onset anchor. Raw duration, adjusted duration, and realized stretch factor are logged for ablation analysis.

\subsection{Chromatic Free Improvisation and Optional Style Constraint Layer}

Chromatic mode leaves pitch class and rhythmic grid unconstrained. The optional \texttt{pentatonic\_\allowbreak 2bar\_\allowbreak 4\_\allowbreak 4} post-processor instead makes a specific style contract explicit; it does not change AMT probabilities.

\subsubsection{Chromatic Mode}

This default retains nucleus sampling and phrase safety controls but applies no hard scale, cadence, meter, or quantization rule.

\subsubsection{Engineering Implementation of the pentatonic\_2bar\_4\_4 Style Constraint Layer}

The base style projection used by A5 starts at the Call-end tick $t_0$ and applies three deterministic stages: (i) truncate to the following eight beats in 4/4; (ii) project pitch to
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{pent}}=\{p\in\mathbb Z:(p\bmod 12)\in M_{\mathrm{mode}}\},
\qquad p_i'=\Pi_{P_{\mathrm{pent}}}(p_i);
\end{equation}
(iii) quantize the Call-end-relative onset to the selected grid while clipping durations to the configured bounds,
\begin{equation}
t_i'=t_0+\max\!\left\{1,\Delta t_{\mathrm{grid}}\operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{\max\{1,t_i-t_0\}}{\Delta t_{\mathrm{grid}}}\right)\right\}.
\end{equation}
A6 adds three theory refinements after this base projection, in implementation order: (iv) replace projected pitches when feasible to respect the configured maximum melodic leap; (v) move notes on beats one and three toward stable degrees (major degrees 1, 3, and 5; minor degrees 1, $\flat3$, and 5); and (vi) replace or append the final cadence toward the configured root or fifth. Strong-beat remapping and cadence run after leap limiting and are not passed through it again, so they have explicit priority over the earlier leap constraint. Equal-distance nearest-pitch choices take the lower MIDI pitch, and grid quantization uses Python's ties-to-even rounding. A5 explicitly disables all three refinements. Because the evaluator's tonal family includes cadence and strong-beat terms targeted only by A6, the family is reported as \emph{constraint-target score}, not ``A5 compliance.''

\subsection{Construction of the Call100 Benchmark}
\label{subsec:call100-dataset}

Call100 contains 100 call phrases and extends early demonstrations into a mixed-distribution development benchmark. It is not a held-out test set: the wrapper rules and reporting choices were iterated with access to this benchmark, and the experiments were not preregistered. First, it reuses Call50 without modifying its files: 10 artificial stress-test calls and 40 real public MIDI fragments. The artificial calls deliberately probe likely failure modes, including clear motifs, minor-pentatonic questions, blues or chromatic tension, half cadences, repeated tails, syncopation, sparse long tones, dense eighth-note motion, arch contours, and large leaps followed by stepwise filling.

The 40 real calls in Call50 are extracted from POP909 \citep{wang2020pop909} using a conservative two-bar melody-sampling procedure. The extraction script scans MIDI tracks with melody-like names such as \texttt{melody}, \texttt{main}, \texttt{vocal}, and \texttt{lead}, while excluding tracks that are more likely to represent accompaniment or percussion, such as \texttt{chord}, \texttt{bass}, and \texttt{drum}. Candidate windows are two bars long, corresponding to 8 beats, and the window advances by 2 beats. A candidate is kept only if it contains between 6 and 24 notes, has a polyphonic-overlap ratio no greater than 0.10, and reaches a major/minor scale-fit score of at least 0.85. A fixed random seed, 20260529, is then used to shuffle the candidates and select 40 real snippets reproducibly.

Second, Call100 copies Call50 into a new benchmark directory and adds 50 public MIDI excerpts without rewriting the original \texttt{calls\_50} directory. In the reproducible run reported here, the available public source was POP909, so all additions come from POP909. The builder constructs two-bar windows from tracks such as \texttt{MELODY}, \texttt{BRIDGE}, and \texttt{PIANO}, computes descriptive features, scores candidates against eight diagnostic target categories, and applies fingerprint deduplication.

Table~\ref{tab:call100-dataset} summarizes the source distribution, Call50 origins, and public-addition targets. The benchmark is diagnostic rather than corpus-representative: it preserves artificial stress cases while adding public excerpts for broader melodic input. Planned MAESTRO, ASAP, Lakh/Slakh, and GiantMIDI sources were not locally available, so the dataset report records their absence and the public additions are backfilled from POP909. The 90 non-artificial Calls trace to 77 unique POP909 song IDs; repeated excerpts from the same song motivate the source-cluster sensitivity analysis in Section~\ref{subsec:statistical-units}. No Call is transposed to the response target key. Only 36 of the 100 manifest rows are labeled C major; accordingly, the fixed C-major response target is a deliberately narrow output contract rather than Call-relative harmonic adaptation.

\subsection{Objective Metric System}
\label{subsec:metrics}

The versioned evaluator \texttt{structural\_compliance\_v1.1} defines the closeness function
\begin{equation}
c(x;a,b)=\operatorname{clip}\left(1-\frac{|x-a|}{b},0,1\right)
\end{equation}
and six component scores:
\begin{align}
\mathcal T &= (\mathrm{PSR}+\mathrm{Cad}+\mathrm{SB})/3,\\
\mathcal R &= (\mathrm{QN}+\mathrm{QRF}+\mathrm{G})/3,\\
\mathcal I &=0.60\,\mathrm{SW}+0.40(1-\mathrm{TSR}),\\
\mathcal P &=\operatorname{clip}[1-2\,\mathrm{CPR}-0.2\max(0,L_{\mathrm{run}}-2),0,1],\\
\mathcal D &=0.55c(H_{\mathrm{pc}};2.0,0.65)+0.45c(U_{\mathrm{pc}};5,2),\\
\mathcal C &=c(Z;0.65,0.35).
\end{align}
PSR, cadence (Cad), and strong-beat stability (SB) use the configured C-major-pentatonic reference; SW is the share of intervals no larger than 3 semitones and TSR the share larger than 7. QN counts durations of at least 0.125 beats; QRF accepts distances no greater than 0.065 beats from \{0.125, 0.25, 0.375, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4\}; G compares successive occupied bars on a 16-slot binary onset grid. $H_{\mathrm{pc}}$ is base-2 pitch-class entropy, $U_{\mathrm{pc}}$ the number of used pitch classes, and $Z$ the zlib/raw-byte ratio of a fixed onset--duration--pitch serialization. Empty responses receive zero on every score. Appendix~\ref{app:evaluator-specification} gives the denominators, serialization, rounding, and empty/single-note edge cases needed to reproduce these values.

The reported structural-compliance composite (SCC; retained as \texttt{objective\_score} in legacy CSV fields) is exactly
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SCC}=0.22\mathcal T+0.20\mathcal R+0.18\mathcal I+0.16\mathcal P+0.14\mathcal D+0.10\mathcal C.
\label{eq:scc}
\end{equation}
To expose the family of terms explicitly manipulated by the style and theory modules, SCC is decomposed as
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{constraint}}=\frac{0.22\mathcal T+0.20\mathcal R}{0.42},\qquad
S_{\mathrm{remaining}}=\frac{0.18\mathcal I+0.16\mathcal P+0.14\mathcal D+0.10\mathcal C}{0.58},
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{SCC}=0.42S_{\mathrm{constraint}}+0.58S_{\mathrm{remaining}}$. $S_{\mathrm{constraint}}$ contains the fixed C-major-pentatonic, cadence, strong-beat, and grid-related terms targeted across A5 and A6. It is retained as \path{style_compliance_score} only in legacy CSV fields. $S_{\mathrm{remaining}}$ is likewise retained as \path{non_style_structural_score}; it is not an independent quality measure because A2 directly targets repetition, A3 changes the symbolic-compression term through onset/duration reshaping, and other controls can affect interval and diversity terms. Both families are manipulation checks for declared output rules. They do not measure motif, contour, harmonic fit, or rhythmic correspondence between Call and response. In particular, a high fixed-C score can coexist with tonal conflict against a non-C Call. The weights and normalizations are fixed code parameters, not learned from listener ratings; SCC must not be interpreted as musical quality \citep{ji2023survey,lerch2025evaluation}.

\subsection{Call--Response Feature Association Audit}
\label{subsec:feature-association-method}

To test limited input--output contingency without human labels, the 27,000 existing response MIDI files are paired back to their Call100 inputs through the frozen answer keys and verified SHA-256 values. Note-On events are grouped by the same start-anchored 80 ms cluster rule used by the endpoint analysis, and each cluster is represented by its median pitch. Three transposition- or duration-normalized similarities are then computed: (i) Pearson correlation between independently centered, unit-normalized 16-point pitch contours resampled by event index; (ii) multiset Dice overlap of adjacent signed interval 2-grams, with each interval clipped to $[-12,12]$ semitones; and (iii) cosine similarity between 16-bin onset-cluster profiles after normalizing each phrase by its own onset span. Short or zero-variance phrases receive NA, and coverage is reported rather than replacing missing values by zero.

For manuscript-only reproduction, a cluster includes onsets no more than 80 ms from its first onset, and an even-sized cluster uses the arithmetic mean of its two middle pitches. Contours require at least three clusters, are linearly interpolated at 16 equally spaced event-index positions, mean-centered, and normalized by their Euclidean ($L_2$) norm; a centered norm no larger than $10^{-12}$ yields NA. Signed intervals are rounded to the nearest integer with Python ties-to-even rounding before clipping, and their multiset Dice score is $2\sum_g\min[n_C(g),n_R(g)]/(\sum_g n_C(g)+\sum_g n_R(g))$; fewer than three clusters yields NA. Onset profiles require at least two clusters and onset span greater than $10^{-12}$ s. An onset $t$ maps to bin $\min\{15,\lfloor16(t-t_0)/(t_{\mathrm{last}}-t_0)\rfloor\}$; the 16 counts are $L_2$-normalized before their dot product is taken.

For each fixed preset, candidate, and trial block containing all 100 Calls, an observed similarity $s_m(C_i,R_i)$ is compared with the valid subset of the other 99 response pairings in that block. Define
\begin{equation}
J_{i,m}=\{j\ne i:s_m(C_i,R_j)\ \text{is defined}\}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
A_m(C_i,R_i)=
\begin{cases}
s_m(C_i,R_i)-\dfrac{1}{|J_{i,m}|}\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J_{i,m}}s_m(C_i,R_j),
& s_m(C_i,R_i)\ \text{defined and }|J_{i,m}|>0,\\
\mathrm{NA}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:association-excess}
\end{equation}
Coverage is the fraction of the 9000 preset--trial--Call rows per candidate for which $A_m$ is defined; it is not coverage of all $100\times100$ matrix cells. The archived dynamic mismatch counts range from 89 to 99 for contour, 95 to 99 for interval 2-grams, and are exactly 99 for onset profiles. Positive association excess therefore means only that the observed pairing shares more of feature $m$ than same-condition mismatches in this finite development benchmark. It does not measure harmonic appropriateness, motif function, musical quality, perceptual contingency, or causal benefit of a control module.

Inference retains each preset--trial block's complete score matrix rather than treating the derived $A_m$ values as fixed. Within a candidate and metric, valid block-level excesses are first averaged within Call and then across 100 equally weighted Calls. For each delete-one-Call replicate, the deleted Call and its response are removed from every block, every retained mismatch mean is rebuilt, and the full aggregation is repeated. With $M=100$ replicates, the classical jackknife standard error and normal interval are
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{SE}_{J}=\sqrt{\frac{M-1}{M}\sum_{d=1}^{M}\left(\widehat\theta_{(-d)}-\overline{\widehat\theta}_{(-\cdot)}\right)^2},
\qquad \operatorname{CI}_{95}=\widehat\theta\pm1.96\operatorname{SE}_{J}.
\label{eq:association-jackknife}
\end{equation}
Candidate contrasts use the same paired deletion and rebuild both candidates' banks. A Call contributes to a contrast only when both candidates' within-Call excesses are defined after block aggregation; this pairwise-valid rule is reapplied after every deletion. The 87-source sensitivity deletes all Calls from one source, rebuilds all retained banks, averages valid Calls within each retained source, and gives retained sources equal weight; Equation~\eqref{eq:association-jackknife} then uses $M=87$. This analysis is deterministic and uses no bootstrap seed or hypothesis-test $p$-values.

The primary mismatch bank includes every other Call in the same condition, so different excerpts from one POP909 song may appear as alternatives. A pre-release sensitivity therefore retains each matched diagonal but removes off-diagonal responses whose source cluster equals that of the focal Call, then rebuilds every bank and both Call/source jackknives. This checks whether within-song alternatives drive the candidate contrast.

\subsection{Statistical Units and Cluster-Aware Inference}
\label{subsec:statistical-units}

The six presets and 15 trials create 90 repeated rows per Call and condition. These rows describe sampling variability but are not 90 independent musical inputs. For SCC candidate summaries, paired candidate/module contrasts, and scheduler quantities, the analysis first averages repeated rows within each of the 100 Calls and then uses 50,000 nonparametric bootstrap resamples, with replacement, of those equally weighted Call means. Paired contrasts are computed within Call before resampling, and intervals are the linearly interpolated 2.5th--97.5th percentiles. The paper-level script uses base seed 20260815 plus fixed, documented offsets; the seed controls Monte Carlo resampling only, not generation. Feature association is different because deleting an input also changes every retained Call's shared mismatch bank. Its uncertainty therefore uses deterministic delete-one Call/source jackknife intervals that rebuild the affected banks, as specified in Section~\ref{subsec:feature-association-method}. Endpoint PS-F1 uses its separately reported 2000-resample Call bootstrap and has no source-cluster reanalysis.

For source sensitivity in the candidate, ablation, scheduler, and feature-association analyses, the 90 public Calls are grouped by their 77 POP909 song IDs while the 10 artificial Calls remain separate, yielding 87 source clusters. Calls are first averaged within source; the sensitivity estimand is the unweighted mean of the 87 source means, so a song with multiple excerpts receives the same weight as a single-Call source. Candidate, ablation, and scheduler source intervals resample those source means with replacement; feature-association source intervals instead delete one source at a time and rebuild all retained mismatch banks. Leave-one-source-out ranges are archived where applicable. No participant-level inference is included in this manuscript.

\subsection{Direct MIDI-VAD Endpoint Benchmark}
\label{subsec:endpoint-benchmark}

The endpoint detector is evaluated by replaying all 100 Call100 files at 5 ms polling resolution. Replay stops at the first Commit, matching the deployed Listening $\rightarrow$ Candidate $\rightarrow$ Commit $\rightarrow$ Inference transition. The final Note-Off of each isolated file is a reproducible proxy boundary. For each post-boundary deadline $d\in\{0.5,1.0,2.0\}$ s, the first Commit is classified as premature ($P$) if earlier than $T_{\mathrm{ref}}-0.1$ s, successful ($S$) if within $[T_{\mathrm{ref}}-0.1,T_{\mathrm{ref}}+d]$, late ($L$) if after that interval, or missed ($M$) if no Commit occurs by the replay horizon $T_{\mathrm{ref}}+15$ s. The custom premature-sensitive first-commit score (PS-F1) sets $TP=S$, $FP=P$, and $FN=P+L+M$, then applies the usual precision/recall harmonic mean. A premature action is counted as both a spurious early Commit and a lost correct turn; a late/missed action is counted only as a failure to Commit within the deadline, not as an additional early action. PS-F1 is therefore an asymmetric safety-oriented engineering score, not standard event-detection F1. The two-second deadline is the primary table, while all deadlines remain in the archive. Signed error is $t_{\mathrm{commit}}-T_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and is summarized only for the $S$ cases; premature, late, and missed cases are excluded from the error distribution. This proxy is structurally mismatched to the detector: its silence clock starts at the latest Note-On, whereas the reference is the final Note-Off. Sustained notes and pedal-held sonorities can therefore alter apparent signed error, and the deployed detector has no active-note or sustain-pedal gate. The benchmark tests reproducible engineering behavior, not a human judgment of the earliest acceptable response onset.

The main conditions compare adaptive MIDI-VAD with clustering/confirmation ablations and fixed 300, 500, and 800 ms silence thresholds. All fixed baselines retain the 80 ms cluster and 150 ms confirmation settings. Table~\ref{tab:endpoint-benchmark} reports 100 calls per condition; PS-F1 intervals use 2000 Call-level bootstrap resamples. For deadline $d$, the independent endpoint script uses Python \texttt{random.Random} seed $20260809+\operatorname{int}(1000d)$ for every condition and reports the linearly interpolated 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles, using position $(B-1)q$ for $B=2000$ ordered replicates. No source-cluster reanalysis was performed for this endpoint benchmark, so its intervals use the 100 Call units only.

\subsection{Runtime Latency Logging and Scheduler Replay}
\label{subsec:latency-logging}

SCC does not characterize scheduler responsiveness. The replay therefore uses local A6 timings captured when \texttt{StreamingAMTGenerator.generate\_events} first yielded a \texttt{GeneratedEvent}. The historical CSV field is named \path{first_token_latency_sec}, but the timed unit is the first complete event object, not a raw vocabulary token and not a transmitted MIDI message. The machine is a Lenovo 82JQ running 64-bit Windows 11 Pro, with an AMD Ryzen 7 5800H (8 cores/16 logical processors), 15.86 GiB physical RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 Laptop GPU with 6144 MiB VRAM. L0 starts decoding at endpoint commit and uses $R_{\mathrm{first}}(0)=\max(80,\tau_{\mathrm{first}})$ ms. L1 overlaps decoding only with the 150 ms confirmation interval and uses Equation~\eqref{eq:perceived-latency-preload}; no additional 250 ms horizon exists. A readiness-target miss occurs when $\tau_{\mathrm{first}}-H>80$ ms. No trial-row hypothesis test is reported because L0 and L1 are deterministic transforms of the same observed timings.

The reported quantity is the commit-anchored first-generated-event readiness lower bound:
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{first}}(H)=\max\left(B,\tau_{\mathrm{first}}-H\right).
\end{equation}
It is an earliest modeled release target relative to endpoint commit. Because the A6 duration/style path may retain the phrase before queueing, and because no queue-send, operating-system MIDI-send, host-receive, or audio-onset timestamp was retained, actual endpoint-to-MIDI and endpoint-to-audio latency are unmeasured and can only be no smaller than this bound under the modeled path.

\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
\label{subsec:subjective-evaluation-scope}

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
The endpoint benchmark uses a final-Note-Off proxy while the detector times silence from the last Note-On. SCC measures rules that the control layers themselves target. The feature-association audit adds contour, interval-2gram, and onset-profile contingency against same-condition mismatches, but it does not measure harmonic fit, semantic answer function, or human-perceived correspondence. The scheduler replay estimates a first-generated-event readiness lower bound, not MIDI transmission, audio onset, or full-stream starvation. These outcomes should remain separate: none is a proxy for perceived conversational timing or musical quality.

\paragraph{Internal and module validity.}
The A0--A6 ablation is cumulative and order-dependent; stepwise increments are conditional on earlier switches. A4 never activates in Call100, and event repair is common but not independently randomized. Speculative-preload values are deterministic replays of observed decoding times, not end-to-end audio-onset measurements. These facts prevent causal claims about fallback quality, standalone repair benefit, or live latency.

\paragraph{Statistical and external validity.}
For candidate, module, feature-association, and scheduler analyses, cluster-aware inference treats 100 Calls as the primary units and 87 sources as sensitivity units; the endpoint analysis has only the 100-Call interval. Association jackknives additionally rebuild the shared mismatch bank. These changes correct trial-row pseudoreplication but do not turn Call100 into a held-out test. The benchmark is dominated by POP909 and uses one checkpoint, one hardware configuration, and one absolute C-major output target. Only 36 Calls are labeled C major, so tonal compatibility with the Call is unknown and may be poor. Overlap with the checkpoint's Lakh-based training data was not audited.

\paragraph{Human evidence and ethics.}
The manuscript deliberately excludes the earlier formative listening data and makes no human-participant claim. It also contains no human-labeled phrase boundaries or live co-performance user study. Consequently, perceived turn taking, performer adaptation, boundary validity, and broader musical usefulness remain open. Any future human evaluation must begin only after prospective institutional review or a documented determination that review is not required.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion-future-work}

The contribution is a reproducible external layer for endpoint candidates, cancellation, phrase controls, and speculative scheduling around a frozen AMT checkpoint. First-commit file replay exposes an endpoint precision--delay trade-off under a non-human proxy. Cluster-aware analysis shows higher declared output compliance for controlled than raw AMT, while the motif baseline remains stronger on SCC; because the score is targeted by the controls, this is a manipulation check rather than musical-quality evidence. A matched--mismatched feature audit adds limited Call conditioning evidence and reveals a trade-off: control increases onset-profile association but reduces interval-2gram association relative to raw AMT, with no positive contour evidence. A 150 ms overlap lowers the modeled commit-anchored first-generated-event readiness bound in replay, but MIDI-send, full-stream, and live audio latency remain unmeasured. Human-labeled boundaries, a held-out multi-source benchmark, key-relative/harmonic correspondence metrics, randomized repair/fallback stress tests, and prospectively reviewed human evaluation are required before stronger interaction or perceptual claims.

\subsection{Exact Evaluator Algorithm}
\label{app:evaluator-specification}

The evaluator merges MIDI tracks, applies tempo changes while accumulating absolute ticks and seconds, and pairs Note-On/Note-Off messages by channel and pitch. A repeated positive-velocity Note-On for an already-active channel/pitch pair replaces the stored onset; unmatched Note-Off messages and notes still active at end of file are discarded. Completed durations are clamped to at least one tick, and notes are sorted by $(\mathrm{onset\ tick},\mathrm{pitch})$ with Python's stable sort preserving completion order for an exact tie. Consequently, simultaneous notes are traversed in ascending pitch order. For $n$ notes, all interval, stepwise, large-jump, and consecutive-repeat fractions use denominator $\max(1,n-1)$. PSR is the fraction of pitches whose C-relative pitch class is in $\{0,2,4,7,9\}$. Cad is 1.00 for a final C, 0.85 for G, 0.70 for another stable degree in $\{0,4,7\}$, 0.45 for another allowed pentatonic degree, and zero otherwise. SB selects notes whose within-bar onset is within $\max(1,\lfloor\mathrm{TPB}/16\rfloor)$ ticks of beats 1 or 3; it is the stable-degree fraction among those notes and equals one when no onset meets the strong-beat test.

QN is the note fraction with duration at least 0.125 beat. QRF is the note fraction within 0.065 beat (inclusive) of any duration in $\{0.125,0.25,0.375,0.5,0.75,1,1.5,2,3,4\}$. For G, each occupied bar is represented by a 16-slot binary onset vector. An onset at tick position $r$ in a bar of $b$ ticks maps to $\operatorname{round}(16r/b)\bmod16$, using Python ties-to-even rounding. G is the mean of $1-d_H/16$ over successive occupied bar indices; it equals one for a single occupied bar and zero only when no bar exists. $H_{\mathrm{pc}}=-\sum_c p_c\log_2p_c$, $U_{\mathrm{pc}}$ counts distinct pitch classes, CPR is the fraction of adjacent equal pitches, and $L_{\mathrm{run}}$ is the longest consecutive equal-pitch run (one for a nonempty single-note response).

For compression, each sorted note is serialized as the UTF-8 text \path{round(onset_ticks/TPB,3):round(duration_ticks/TPB,3):pitch}; items are joined by one ASCII space in evaluation order. $Z$ is \texttt{len(zlib.compress(raw))/len(raw)} using the Python zlib default compression settings. Python's ties-to-even rounding and number-to-string conversion are therefore part of the score definition. The archived generation/evaluation provenance did not record Python or zlib versions; a current revalidation under Python 3.12.10 and zlib runtime 1.3.1 reproduced the stored scores within the tolerance reported in Section~\ref{subsec:evaluation-integrity}, but does not identify the original runtime. A zero-note response bypasses all submetric calculations and receives zero for SCC and both component-family scores. For a single note, the transition numerator is zero and denominator is one; G and no-strong-beat SB are one. These conventions, the constants in Table~\ref{tab:evaluator-parameters}, and Equation~\eqref{eq:scc} fully specify \texttt{structural\_compliance\_v1.1} apart from that archived runtime-version gap.

The remaining implementation edge cases are as follows. If the clustered-onset count is $K_{\mathrm{on}}<2$, or if $u_{K_{\mathrm{on}}}\leq u_1$, endpoint density is $\mu_{\min}=0.25$ s$^{-1}$. If duration shaping receives one note, $i/\max(1,n-1)=0$. With zero generated events, the ablation runner bypasses duration shaping: A3 retains the empty response for evaluation, whereas A4--A6 enter their variant-specific motif-fallback path; the shaping helper itself returns an empty list, zero raw/adjusted durations, and unit scale if called directly. Endpoint replay terminates after its first Commit; a premature first Commit cannot be repaired by a later decision. A4 fallback retains free/chromatic style, A5 applies only the base style projection, and A6 adds theory refinements. The Note-On detector does not track active-note or sustain-pedal state, so held sonorities remain an explicit limitation rather than an implicit boundary rule.
